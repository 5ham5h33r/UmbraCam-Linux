## Repeating the stream tests

Build with `scripts\build-native.ps1`, then use Windows PowerShell:

```powershell
.\scripts\test-camera-streams.ps1 -Camera normal
.\scripts\test-camera-streams.ps1 -Camera component
# Only after installation and with UmbraCam running:
.\scripts\test-camera-streams.ps1 -Camera virtual
```

The runner enforces a 20-second deadline and terminates only its own test
process if capture hangs. stdout/stderr logs are kept under the ignored
`build` directory. The virtual stream test alone does not distinguish IR
content from the component's placeholder: final validation must also confirm
that the publisher updates the shared frame transport.

Testing uncovered a PowerShell process exit-code handling problem. The runner
and installer now cache child process handles before waiting so the real exit
code remains available after a fast process exits.

## Flicker diagnosis and fix

The 90-frame raw-sensor diagnostic reported L8 at 15 fps, with exactly 45
illuminated frames alternating with 45 ambient frames. Exposure and power-line
frequency controls are unsupported. The initial measurement's adjacent-frame
brightness difference was 19.863 intensity levels versus a lag-two difference
of 0.314, strongly matching alternating exposure phases.

On a second 90-frame sample, selecting illuminated frames from that **same
input sequence** reduced the mean adjacent brightness difference from 19.977
to 0.170 (about 99.1%). These are whole-frame brightness metrics, not a measured
reduction of spatial banding. No raw images were saved and no exposure,
illumination or driver settings were changed.

The corrected publisher passed the installed virtual-camera stream test
(15 delivered samples, 11 changing checksums, 14 advancing timestamps). Its
shared-memory counter advanced by 30 fresh frames in four seconds, matching
7.5 illuminated images per second. The clean-stop command succeeded and the
physical IR probe reacquired the sensor afterward.

The policy tests cover alternating illumination, ambient-only streams, missing
metadata, recovery when illumination stops, and resumption when it returns.
The publisher also ignores duplicate source timestamps and offers an explicit
`--all-ir-frames` comparison mode.

Illumination classification uses the driver's documented
[InfraredMediaFrame.IsIlluminated property](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/uwp/api/windows.media.capture.frames.infraredmediaframe.isilluminated).
